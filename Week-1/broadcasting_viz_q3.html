e = torch.zeros(8, 1, 6, 1)   f = torch.zeros(7, 1, 5)   →   result is (8, 7, 6, 5), verified with torch.

Outlined cells are the ones that get stretched. The rule per column: sizes must be equal, or one of them must be 1 (a missing leading dim counts as 1). The result takes the non-1 size. Neither tensor "wins" as a whole. Each column is decided on its own.

2. Why (8, 1, 6, 1) was wrong

Predicted
(8, 1, 6, 1)

This keeps e's shape untouched, as if the higher-rank tensor sets the output. But a size-1 dim never survives when the other side has size > 1. Dims −3 and −1 of e are 1, and f has 7 and 5 there, so they must expand.

Actual
(8, 7, 6, 5)

Two dims come from e (8, 6) and two come from f (7, 5). The output has 8·7·6·5 = 1680 elements, far more than either input (48 and 35). Broadcasting only ever grows shapes, never keeps a 1 that the other side disagrees with.

3. What the stretching looks like (one slice)

Fix dim −4 = 0 and dim −2 = 0 and look at the remaining plane (dim −3 × dim −1). In that plane e is a single value, f is a 7 × 5 grid, and the sum is the full 7 × 5 grid. The same picture repeats for every (8 × 6) choice of the fixed dims.
